Explore the OpenAI supply-chain geography map in Google My Maps

Text overview

The map organizes research into three layers: data centers, raw materials, and headquarters/data labeling. The accompanying reflection discusses the geographic spread of infrastructure and the difficulty of tracing specific mines to OpenAI. The text below preserves the submitted research; indirect supplier and investment links should not be read as verified material deliveries to OpenAI.

Research reflection

For my research, I specifically only did locations that tie back to OpenAI. I separated the map into 3 layers: Data Centers, Raw Materials, and HQ/ Data Labeling. The sources I used for all of the Data centers were found by looking through OpenAI’s website. The data centers in general were all very easy to research, considering all the ones on my map were posted publicly. One thing I learned about this was that OpenAI partners with Oracle, SoftBank, and hardware companies like Nvidia on a project called Stargate. All of these companies share multiple data centers across the world. The hardest thing is to track down the supply chain of raw materials used by OpenAI. Almost all of OpenAI’s hardware is outsourced; therefore, they do not publicly disclose the extraction sites that they use. For example, I know that almost all of OpenAI’s hardware chips come from Nvidia; I then researched where Nvidia gets its silicon and Quartz to make its chips. I then found out that Nvidia gets these materials from mines in Spruce Pine, North Carolina. Another example of this is learning that OpenAI’s founder has heavily invested in a company called Kobold Metals, which is a big source for copper and lithium. Kobold Metals' biggest project was located in Zambia; therefore, I pinned the site on my map. What surprised me most about this research was how difficult it was to connect individual mining operations to the companies that ultimately use their materials. While OpenAI publicly announces many of their data center projects, the supply chains behind the hardware are much less transparent. I realised how difficult it can be to determine exactly where a company gets its resources from. In most of my research, I would first check OpenAI’s website to see where they get their hardware from. Then I would research where that company gets its raw materials.

The main argument of my map is that AI relies on a much larger physical infrastructure than most people realize. Even though OpenAI is headquartered in Silicon Valley, they rely heavily on data centers, raw materials, and human labor from all around the world. My layers on my map show how the development of AI involves much more than just software computer programs, and that AI is not just in the “cloud”. One of the biggest patterns my map shows is how geographically spread out the infrastructure is. The resources and facilities supporting the company extend to countries beyond just the US and certainly extend beyond Silicon Valley. OpenAI uses sources such as Zambia, Australia, China, and Norway, demonstrating how AI’s development depends on a global network of industries and workers. This also made me wonder if the people who are putting in the manual labor in these raw materials extraction sites are even benefiting from AI’s technology. My map connects to the ideas in Data Feminism, specifically the importance of examining power and making overlooked information visible. Companies frequently advertise their advancements in AI; however, there is not much attention given to the physical mining operations, energy consumption, and workers that make up the system. The locations I included on my map conflict with the idea that AI exists only online or in the cloud. My biggest limitation with my map is that I could not directly trace every raw material back to OpenAI. If I had access to more detailed supply chain information, I would be able to pin more locations. The source of the raw materials depends on who they are partnering with or the market price at the time of purchase; therefore, the exact source of materials changes frequently.
