What the network leaves out

Workers, mining locations, affected communities, water consumption, and environmental costs are largely absent. Corporate visibility should not be confused with the full range of contributions and consequences behind AI.

Network analysis

This network maps the relationships behind OpenAI’s computing infrastructure. It includes 27 nodes and 38 edges connecting companies, infrastructure projects, and data center sites. The relationships include investment, technology partnerships, cloud services, chip fabrication, memory supply, and facility development.

NVIDIA is the most connected node, with 15 incident edges linking it to 12 distinct neighbors. Stargate follows with 13 edges and 11 neighbors. OpenAI has seven edges connecting it to six neighbors. Counting distinct neighbors matters because some companies have multiple relationships. For example, Samsung connects to NVIDIA through both fabrication and memory supply. These counts describe this selected dataset, rather than proving which organization has the greatest power across the entire AI industry.

Two major groups emerge. One centers on Stargate, connecting investors and technology partners with facilities. The other centers on NVIDIA, linking chip production, memory manufacturers, hardware assembly, and systems integration. OpenAI’s cloud relationships form another branch. NVIDIA’s connection to Stargate helps join the manufacturing and infrastructure groups.

The surprising result is that OpenAI does not have the highest degree, even though the project focuses on its ecosystem. The network makes supporting infrastructure more visible than a familiar product name might suggest. However, this result also reflects which relationships were collected.

Several important actors are missing: mining workers, factory employees, construction crews, data workers, and communities near facilities. Water consumption, environmental costs, and labor conditions are also absent. A node with few recorded connections may still be essential.

Finally, NVIDIA’s supplier links describe its broader supply chain, not confirmed deliveries to particular OpenAI facilities. The arrows follow the dataset’s ordering and should not all be read as material flows. These limits show why a network needs explanation: visible connections reveal dependencies, but missing connections can conceal both human contributions and the uneven distribution of costs and benefits.

Artist statement

Behind OpenAI explores the global companies and infrastructure that make artificial intelligence possible. I wanted to move beyond the experience of typing into a chatbot and examine the relationships supporting that interaction. The network brings investors, cloud providers, chip companies, manufacturers, and data center projects into the same frame.

The main argument is that AI depends on concentrated networks of resources and coordination. NVIDIA and Stargate stand out because they connect many of the selected entities. OpenAI remains important, but its position shows that a recognizable AI company relies on organizations outside its own boundaries. The larger highlighted nodes direct attention to these actors without claiming that size measures their wealth or moral importance.

Different line colors distinguish kinds of relationships. This matters because investing in a project is different from manufacturing hardware or supplying memory. Treating every connection as identical would hide how these organizations exercise different forms of influence.

The network also exposes the limits of its own perspective. Corporate names are easy to identify in public announcements, while workers and affected communities are much harder to see. Their absence does not mean their contributions or experiences are unimportant. It reflects the boundaries of the dataset and the sources used to construct it.

A network centered on labor, water, mineral extraction, or community organizing would tell a different story. Companies appearing central here might become distant endpoints. I want viewers to notice that visualization involves choices about whose relationships count, whose names appear, and whose experiences remain outside.
